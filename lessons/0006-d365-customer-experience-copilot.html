Where Copilot lives in the D365 CX apps

Copilot in Dynamics 365 Customer Service is enabled in the Customer Service admin center > Copilot. Before any of it works, four prerequisites must hold: licensing, security roles, the Customer Service workspace, and grounding data worth reading. An exam stem that says "users can't see Copilot" is often a prerequisite question in disguise — check licensing and security roles before touching configuration. [source, source]

Business terms: the vocabulary Copilot reads

Business terms are your organization's standardized vocabulary, stored in Dataverse fields, option sets, and classification metadata. Copilot reads them when it produces summaries and recommendations — which means every inconsistency in your vocabulary leaks straight into the AI's output. The problem business terms solve: vague, inconsistent summaries cause agents to lose trust in Copilot, and a Copilot that agents double-check against the raw transcript is negative value. [source, source]

StandardizeExamples
Product namesThe official names Copilot should use — not the three nicknames support teams invented
Service categoriesThe canonical category list summaries should draw from
Internal terminologySLA tiers, channel names
Department namesConsistent org references in summaries and recommendations
Case classification & outcome termsThe classification values and resolution labels Copilot propagates

Managing summary fields. You control which fields Copilot's summaries read — include or exclude fields, and map custom fields into the summary. The canonical example: mapping a custom "Issue Type" field so summaries classify cases in your terms. The exam-critical failure mode is mis-mapping: wire the field wrong and you get misinterpreted categories, outdated terminology, and wrong next steps — that exact triad. When a scenario shows those three symptoms after a field change, the answer is the summary field mapping, not the model. [source]

Best practices: consistent naming across departments; avoid obscure abbreviations; align terms with the CRM classification fields; review terms regularly as the business evolves; make terms reflect customer language, not internal shorthand; and assign ownership per department so the vocabulary has a maintainer rather than a committee.

Conversation summary formats: paragraph vs structured

ParagraphStructured
What it isA narrative prose summaryPredefined sections: Customer Issue, Actions Taken, Pending Items, Next Steps, Resolution Status
Choose whenQuick escalations — a human needs the story fastDocumentation standards, regulated industries, consistency across every record

Format is how the summary is written; surfacing is who sees it where. Outputs can be surfaced on custom case forms, agent dashboards, and role-specific forms — Tier 1 agents, escalation agents, and supervisors each get a view matched to their job. The exam separates these two decisions: a scenario that needs identical sections on every record is a format question (structured); a scenario that needs supervisors to see different fields is a surfacing question (role-specific forms). Every Copilot-in-D365 design decision lands in one of four areas. Learn the table cold — "which customization area solves X" is a direct exam pattern. [source]

Prompt/outputTone, structure, required fields, sensitive terms to avoidWhat must every output contain — and what must it never say?

Best practices that govern all four: start with agent workflows (design from what agents actually do, not from what the settings page offers); keep terms consistent; review outputs for tone, accuracy, and brand; limit data scope to what the task needs; update prompts as strategy evolves; and keep human oversight of AI content before it reaches records or customers.

Custom connectors for Copilot in D365 Sales

A custom connector bridges an external API ↔ Dynamics 365 Sales ↔ Copilot Studio actions (production-ready preview). The mechanics below are the most exam-quoted facts in this lesson — treat them as memorization-grade. [source]

The environment rule. The connector must be created in an environment with Dynamics 365 apps enabled. The default environment is not supported. A connector built in the default environment is simply unusable for this scenario — no setting fixes it after the fact.

StepWhereWhat you provide
1. Build the connectorPower Apps / Power AutomateAPI host, base URL, security settings
2. Define operationsThe connector definitionAn uploaded OpenAPI definition
3. Wire authenticationMicrosoft Entra IDOAuth 2.0 + two app registrations; set Enable onbehalfOf login = true so plugins get OBO tokens — the backend receives Entra tokens as the calling seller
4. Create the actionCopilot StudioA Copilot action from the connector; configure its data
5. PublishCopilot StudioPublish the action
6. Enable for usersAdminAdmin enables the action for Sales users

Why two app registrations? The on-behalf-of flow propagates the calling user's identity end to end: the seller's permissions reach the external API, so the connector acts as the seller, not as an app identity. That is the OBO pattern from your glossary, delivered here through a pair of Entra app registrations with Enable onbehalfOf login switched on. The exam will describe per-user delegation to an external API and ask what the auth setup requires — this is it.

The 7-day propagation gotcha. After publish, actions may take up to 7 days to appear for Sales users. Having users sign out and back in accelerates availability. So "published Monday, invisible Wednesday" is expected behavior — sign out/in first, and only treat it as a defect after the 7-day window passes.

Governance. Review third-party terms and privacy implications before connecting — the connector makes external data accessible inside Microsoft 365 experiences. Optionally pursue Microsoft certification for org-wide availability of the connector.

Designing agents for D365 Contact Center

Contact Center design is per-channel: each channel gets its own AI capabilities, and choosing which capability fits which channel is the tested judgment. [source]

ChannelAI capabilities
VoiceReal-time summarization, transcription, intent extraction
Live ChatSuggested replies, knowledge retrieval, escalation triggers
Digital Messaging (SMS/WhatsApp)Conversational flows, routing, sentiment
Omnichannel widgetEmbedded assistant in the agent experience

Agent context is structured data — expose it least-privilege

The agent's context is not a document; it is structured data: customer identity, case history, channel, transcript, routing queues and priority, and skills. The entity types are Customer, Case, Product, Subscription; example fields include CaseID, CustomerType, Channel, IssueCategory, SentimentScore. The design rule: expose only the attributes the task needs. Every extra attribute costs you on three axes — accuracy (the model weighs irrelevant fields), noise (longer, muddier context), and privacy (data the agent never needed is data it can leak). This is lesson 2's least-privilege instinct applied to context design. [source]

Features, enablement, and guardrails

Copilot features in Contact Center: conversation summaries, suggested actions, knowledge retrieval, customer insights, automated drafting. Enablement is two-dimensional — per environment and per user role: you decide which environments get Copilot and which roles see which features. Design principles: maintain omnichannel awareness (the agent knows the channel it's serving); align capabilities to workstreams (voice, messaging, persistent chat); set guardrails — no unsupported actions, safe language, explicit escalation rules; and keep data least-privilege throughout. [source]

Voice mode and IVR design

Voice is the channel where conversational design rules have teeth — a chat user can skim a long answer, a caller cannot. Use cases: customer-service IVR, field technician support, routing, triage, and self-service (HR/IT, facilities). [source, source]

The design rules — each one is a quiz distractor waiting to be violated:

  • Short, clear phrasing — callers can't reread; every extra sentence is cognitive load.
  • No long multi-sentence responses — break information into turns; never recite paragraphs.
  • Confirmation steps — before acting, play back what you heard ("You'd like to move the appointment to Friday — is that right?").
  • Design for interruptions and backtracking — callers barge in and change their minds; the flow must survive both.
  • Confidence checks before actions — low confidence means clarify or escalate, never guess-and-execute.

The canonical voice flow: speak → interpret → confirm/clarify → execute → concise spoken output. Note what is not in this lesson: reasoning modes. Standard vs deep reasoning was covered in lesson 5 and applies here unchanged — voice design is about the conversational contract above, not the reasoning tier underneath it.

Check yourself

1. A regulated service org must produce audit-ready conversation records with identical sections on every escalation. Which summary format should you configure?

Documentation standards and regulated industries are the stated trigger for the structured format — Customer Issue, Actions Taken, Pending Items, Next Steps, Resolution Status. Options a and c describe the narrative format; d conflates surfacing (who sees it) with format (how it's written), and restricting to supervisors doesn't make records audit-ready.

2. Right after a custom "Issue Type" field was wired into Copilot's summaries, agents report misinterpreted categories, outdated terminology, and wrong next steps. Most likely cause?

Misinterpreted categories, outdated terminology, and wrong next steps are the documented triad of a mis-mapped summary field — and the symptom appeared immediately after the field was wired in. Data scope that is merely broad would add noise, not systematically wrong categories.

3. Leadership complains that Copilot's case summaries draw on fields and timeline entries the service team considers noise. Which customization area fixes this?

Case fields, CRM entities, knowledge sources, and timeline elements are the contents of the data-scope dial. Business terms would fix wrong names, prompt output would fix wrong shape, surface integration would fix wrong placement — none of them removes noisy inputs.

4. A maker builds a custom connector for Copilot in Dynamics 365 Sales inside the tenant's default environment, and it cannot be used. Why?

The connector must be created in an environment with Dynamics 365 apps enabled, and the default environment is explicitly not supported — it has to be rebuilt in the right environment. Certification is optional (for org-wide availability), and an OpenAPI definition is a build step, not the environment defect described.

5. Sellers must act on an external API as themselves through a Copilot in Sales plugin. Which authentication setup is required?

"As themselves" demands per-user delegation: OAuth 2.0 + Microsoft Entra ID with two app registrations and Enable onbehalfOf login = true, so the backend receives Entra tokens for the calling seller via OBO. One registration without delegation makes the app act as itself; keys and basic auth carry no per-user identity.

6. An admin publishes a Copilot Studio action for Sales on Monday. By Wednesday sellers still cannot see it. What should you check first?

Actions can take up to 7 days to appear after publish, and signing out/in accelerates availability — so day two is inside the normal window and the sign-out check is the first move. Treat it as a defect only after the 7-day window passes.

7. A contact center wants real-time transcription, live summarization, and intent extraction during phone calls. Which channel's AI capabilities deliver this?

Real-time summarization, transcription, and intent extraction are the voice channel's stated capability set. Live chat owns suggested replies and escalation triggers; digital messaging owns conversational flows, routing, and sentiment; the widget is the embedded assistant surface, not the phone-call pipeline.

8. You are designing agent context for a chat agent that only verifies subscription status. What should the context include?

Expose only needed attributes — every extra field degrades accuracy (the model weighs irrelevant data), adds noise, and widens the privacy surface. Full records and complete histories are the over-exposure failure mode this rule exists to prevent.

9. A voice IVR greets callers with a three-sentence recital of every menu option before accepting input. Which design rule does this violate?

Short, clear phrasing — and its corollary, no long multi-sentence responses — is the rule a spoken wall of text breaks; callers can't skim. Confirmation and confidence checks govern actions (none taken here), and interruption support is about surviving barge-in, not about greeting length.

Apply it: three D365 CX designs

Decide each before opening the reveal.

A. A health insurer (regulated, 400-seat service org) is enabling Copilot in D365 Customer Service. Summaries will feed audit-reviewed case records; Tier 1 agents, escalation agents, and supervisors need different views; leadership wants tight control over which data Copilot may read. Design the configuration.

Your design?

Prereqs first: licensing, security roles, Customer Service workspace, grounding data — then enable features in Customer Service admin center > Copilot. Business terms: standardize plan/product names, service categories, SLA tiers, channel names, and case classification/outcome terms; align them with the CRM classification fields; avoid obscure abbreviations; assign ownership per department and review regularly. Summary fields: include/exclude deliberately and map any custom fields (like "Issue Type") correctly — mis-mapping is what produces misinterpreted categories and wrong next steps. Format: structured — audit-ready records with identical sections (Customer Issue, Actions Taken, Pending Items, Next Steps, Resolution Status) are the regulated-industry pattern. Data scope: limited to the case fields, entities, and knowledge sources the workflows need. Prompt/output: required fields per summary plus sensitive terms to avoid. Surface integration: role-specific forms for Tier 1, escalation, and supervisors. Oversight: human review of AI content before it enters the audited record. [source, source]

B. A sales org's architect reports: "We built the connector in Power Apps last week, published the Copilot Studio action yesterday, and no seller can see it. Also, our security review wants to know how the external API receives user identity." Give an ordered answer.

Your answer?

Work the chain in order. (1) Environment: confirm the connector was created in an environment with Dynamics 365 apps enabled — if it was built in the default environment, it must be rebuilt; nothing else will fix it. (2) Auth: OAuth 2.0 + Microsoft Entra ID with two app registrations and Enable onbehalfOf login = true — that is how the API receives Entra tokens as the calling seller (OBO delegation), which answers the security reviewer. (3) Publish chain: Copilot Studio action created → data configured → published → admin enabled it for Sales users — the last step is often the missing one. (4) Timing: published yesterday is well inside the up-to-7-day propagation window — have sellers sign out and back in, and only escalate after 7 days. (5) Governance: confirm the third-party terms/privacy review happened, since connector data becomes accessible inside M365 experiences. [source]

C. A contact center wants a voice agent that schedules service appointments and routes callers, with human agents available for anything sensitive. Design the voice experience and the context the agent receives.

Your design?

Use case fit: customer-service IVR plus routing — squarely the voice pattern. Conversation flow: speak → interpret → confirm/clarify → execute → concise spoken output. Design rules: short, clear prompts; no long multi-sentence responses; a confirmation step before booking anything ("Friday at 2 pm — shall I confirm?"); design for interruptions and backtracking (callers change their minds mid-sentence); confidence checks before actions — low confidence triggers clarification, not execution. Escalation: an explicit rule handing sensitive requests or repeated clarification failures to a human agent. Context: least-privilege — Customer identity and the Subscription/appointment fields the scheduling task needs (CaseID, CustomerType, Channel), not full case history or marketing data; every extra attribute costs accuracy, adds noise, and widens privacy exposure. Workstream alignment: configure the capabilities on the voice workstream, with omnichannel awareness so the agent knows it's on a call, not in chat. [source, source]
