Extend, configure, or build

Choosing the agent platform — the decision AB-100 tests most

AB-100 is not a product-trivia exam. Most scenario questions reduce to one judgment: given this business need, which rung of the agent ladder do you stop at? Microsoft's own guidance is explicit about the order: start with a SaaS (prebuilt) agent, escalate to extending Microsoft 365 Copilot, then Copilot Studio, then code-first in Microsoft Foundry — and only touch GPUs/containers when nothing higher holds. [source, CAF technology plan]

The decision rules that matter

1. SaaS-first principle. Always open with: does a prebuilt agent meet the functional requirements? If yes — stop. Custom builds carry governance, evaluation, and ops burden you must justify. [source]

2. Extend M365 Copilot when: the interaction lives in M365 apps, the data lives in M365 (Graph, semantic index), productivity is the goal, and actions stay simple. Built-in compliance and Responsible AI come free.

3. Build in Copilot Studio when: you need a purpose-built agent (business process automation, publishing to Teams/web/D365 channels, autonomous triggers, agent flows) but still want a governed, low-code, fast loop that business users can co-own. Constraints: connector-scoped data, platform-enforced guardrails, medium risk tier. [source]

4. Go Foundry when: multi-agent orchestration, custom reasoning, proprietary models, deep system integration, or explicit evaluation pipelines are required. You gain control; you inherit governance, monitoring, failover, and cost. [source]

5. Single agent before multi-agent. Go multi-agent only when you cross security/compliance boundaries, have multiple owning teams, or a roadmap spanning 3–5+ functions. Persona switching and better retrieval solve many "multi-agent" needs more cheaply.

Check yourself

1. A sales org wants CRM-grounded assistance inside Outlook and Teams — meeting prep, email summaries, opportunity updates — with zero custom development. Best fit?

Prebuilt SaaS agent meets the requirement exactly — SaaS-first principle. Copilot for Sales is purpose-built for sellers in Outlook/Teams with CRM grounding. Anything custom is unjustified cost and governance burden.

2. HR wants a self-service policy Q&A agent grounded in SharePoint, published to Teams, and maintained by business users — not developers.

A purpose-built retrieval agent maintained by non-developers is Copilot Studio's sweet spot: SharePoint knowledge sources, Teams channel, governed SaaS. No custom model is needed — general summarization/Q&A over knowledge is what catalog models already do well.

3. A firm needs a bespoke multi-agent system: custom orchestration across proprietary tools, strict evaluation gates, and agents that cross a compliance boundary between two departments.

Crossing a compliance boundary is an explicit trigger for multi-agent design, and custom orchestration + evaluation pipelines are Foundry's remit. MCP extends tool reach but doesn't give you pro-code orchestration and custom evals.

4. Knowledge workers want help drafting and summarizing inside Word and Teams. Data lives entirely in SharePoint and email. Minimal engineering investment is a hard constraint.

Interaction in M365 apps + data in M365 + productivity goal + simple actions = extend Copilot. You inherit Microsoft's Responsible AI guardrails and skip custom governance entirely.

5. An architect kicks off design for a new agentic business process. Per Microsoft's technology planning guidance, what is the first question to answer?

The CAF agent technology plan is a decision tree whose root is SaaS-fit. Platform capabilities and team skills only matter after a prebuilt agent has been ruled out.

Apply it: Fabrikam, three needs

Fabrikam (manufacturing, 5,000 employees, D365 + M365 estate) asks you to place three initiatives on the ladder. Decide each before opening the reveal.

A. Contact-center reps need case summaries, conversation recaps, and next-step suggestions inside the Dynamics 365 Customer Service workspace.

Your call?

Configure prebuilt Copilot in Dynamics 365 Customer Service. Every capability named is out-of-box: case/conversation summaries, suggested actions, knowledge retrieval. The design work is business terms, field mapping, and summary format — configuration, not construction. [source]

B. Field ops wants an agent that fires when a Dataverse row is created, runs a multi-step approval workflow, posts to Teams, and is maintained by the ops team itself.

Your call?

Copilot Studio autonomous agent. Event triggers, agent flows, Teams publishing, business-owned maintenance — all native to Copilot Studio. Not M365-extensible (event-driven autonomy), not Foundry (no custom orchestration or models). [source]

C. R&D wants a multi-agent research system over proprietary formulations data, with custom fine-tuned models, private networking, and an explicit evaluation pipeline with release gates.

Your call?

Microsoft Foundry — and if full isolation/BYO hosting is non-negotiable, GPUs & containers. Custom models, deep orchestration, and owned eval pipelines are exactly what you trade speed for at the bottom of the ladder. [source]
